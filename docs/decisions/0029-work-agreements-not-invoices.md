# 0029. Work agreements, accepted by re-authentication

- **Date:** 2026-09-18

## Context

A creative and a client settle terms in the thread — what is included, how long
it takes, what it costs — and then nothing holds it. The agreement exists as
scrollback. Either side can remember it differently a month later, and the
messages around it can be read either way.

Offers already attach to messages ([0024](./0024-offers-attach-to-messages.md)),
so the thread is where commercial context lives. What is missing is a document
the two of them can point at, and a moment where the client says yes to a
specific version of it.

## Decision

**It is an agreement, not an invoice.** This is the load-bearing part of the
name. In the Philippines an invoice is a BIR-regulated instrument — it carries a
TIN, registered serial numbers, and an authority to print. A creative who sends
a client a document headed "Invoice", generated by us, is making a claim about
their tax registration that we have not checked and cannot check. The exposure
is theirs, not Bilikha's, which is worse rather than better.

What is described here has no tax character: no money moves through Bilikha, and
nothing is receipted. It is a statement of scope, schedule and price that one
party proposes and the other accepts. "Agreement" says that and claims nothing
else. Real invoicing, if it is ever wanted, is a separate thing built on top,
and it starts with asking the creative for a TIN.

**The creative issues it; the client accepts it.** A client cannot draft one. It
is the creative's quote for their own work, and letting either side author one
doubles the states for no gain.

**Line items, and the total is computed.** Each service in the package is its
own row with its own price in integer centavos. The total is the sum, derived at
read time. It is never stored beside the lines, for the same reason a cached
count drifts from its rows — a stored total that disagrees with its line items
is a document that argues with itself in front of a client.

**Start date and duration; the end date is computed.** Duration, start and end
are three facts where two suffice. Storing all three guarantees they eventually
disagree, and the one people will act on is whichever the UI happened to render.

**A revision is a new version, not an edit.** The client's two choices are
*accept* and *request changes*. Requesting changes does not let the client edit
the creative's document — it sends a note back. The creative then issues version
2, and version 1 becomes `superseded`. Nothing is ever mutated in place and
nothing is deleted, so the thread holds the whole negotiation.

**Acceptance freezes the version it names.** Once accepted, that version is
immutable: no new line items, no date changes, no withdrawal. An agreement the
issuer can still edit after acceptance is not an agreement.

**Acceptance requires the client's password, and stores none of it.** The client
re-enters their password to accept. This is step-up authentication — the same
reason a password is asked again before changing an email address — and it
exists to make acceptance a deliberate act by the account holder rather than a
misplaced tap by whoever picked up the phone.

What is recorded is an acceptance: the accepting user's id, the exact timestamp,
and a SHA-256 hash of the canonical content of the version accepted. No
password, no derived key, no hash of a credential goes anywhere near the
document. The content hash is what carries the weight — it is what proves the
document was not edited after the fact, which is the actual question in a
dispute.

**The client posts back the hash it displayed.** If it does not match what the
server computes now, acceptance is refused and the client is asked to re-read.
Otherwise a client can accept what was on screen while something else is in the
database.

**Stated plainly in the product: this is not a legal signature and not escrow.**
The acceptance screen says so. Bilikha holds no money, releases none, and
guarantees no outcome. Implying otherwise to people choosing who to trust with
work is the worst thing this feature could do.

**It is a record with its own address, not only a message.** The thread is where
an agreement is proposed and accepted, and the card belongs there — that is the
context the terms were negotiated in. But a message is a position in a stream,
and a record needs to be reachable without re-reading the stream that produced
it. So an agreement also has a permalink, and both parties get an index of
theirs.

The index is a third segment in History, beside *Inquired* and *Saved*, mirrored
by mode like everything else there — a creative sees agreements they issued, a
client sees agreements they hold. It is not a fifth item in the bottom bar: four
is already tight at 346px, and History is precisely the "what has happened"
surface this belongs to.

**Schedule labels are derived from dates; work status is not invented.** The
index can honestly say a job starts on the 3rd or ended on the 14th, because
those are stored. It must not show *Completed* or *In progress* — nobody
maintains that, and a stale "In progress" is read as a fact about the work. Same
objection as payment status, for the same reason.

**Participants only.** The agreement is visible to the two people in the thread.
Administrators reach it only through an existing report, exactly as conversation
contents already work. Suspension needs no special handling — the per-request
check ([0028](./0028-suspension-is-enforced-per-request.md)) already stops a
suspended account reading or acting on anything.

**A third nullable foreign key on `messages`, deliberately.** `messages` already
carries `offer_id` and `posting_id`; `agreement_id` joins them. A single
polymorphic attachment table looks tidier and is not — it cannot hold a real
foreign key to three different tables, so it trades referential integrity for
the appearance of symmetry. Three columns keep every attachment provably
pointing at a row that exists. **Four is the line.** A fourth attachment type
means stopping and designing this properly rather than adding another column.

## Alternatives considered

**Call it an invoice, as asked.** Rejected on the BIR problem above. The flow is
unchanged either way, so the name costs nothing to get right and is expensive to
change once creatives have sent them.

**Type your full name to accept, instead of a password.** Phishing-safe, and it
avoids training people to enter passwords outside a login form — a real cost of
the chosen design. Rejected because a typed name proves only that someone knew
the name, which is printed on the document. The password at least binds
acceptance to the account. The training risk is mitigated by the prompt living
inside the app on an authenticated screen and never arriving by link.

**A real e-signature integration.** Correct if this ever needs to hold up in
court. Rejected for now: it is a paid third party, it needs identity documents,
and it is disproportionate to a ₱5,000 poster commission in Naval.

**Let the client edit the document and send it back.** Fewer round trips.
Rejected: it makes authorship ambiguous, so "who agreed to what" needs a per-
field audit trail to answer. Versions answer it with one column.

**Store the total.** Rejected above. It would need a check constraint against
the sum of its lines to be safe, at which point it is the sum of its lines.

**Payment schedules — deposit, balance, milestones.** Rejected for this round.
Bilikha cannot see payments, so tracking them would show a status nobody
maintains, and people would read an unpaid milestone as a fact about the money.

## Consequences

**Good.** Two people have one thing to point at, and it names a version. The
negotiation stays in the thread it happened in. Nothing about money movement is
claimed, so nothing about money movement can be got wrong.

**Good.** Append-only versions mean a dispute is answerable from the data: who
sent what, when, and which version carries an acceptance.

**Bad.** It trains password entry on a screen that is not the login form. That
is a real phishing surface, and it is the strongest argument against the design.
The mitigation is that the prompt is only ever reached from inside an
authenticated thread — so any *link* asking for a Bilikha password is
illegitimate by construction, which is worth saying in the help text.

**Bad.** People will read "agreement" as more binding than it is. The disclaimer
helps and will not fully work. The alternative — no such feature — leaves them
relying on scrollback, which is worse.

**Bad.** A third attachment column, and a `messageAttachmentFields` that now
branches three ways.

**Watch for.** Creatives issuing version after version to walk a price up, or
clients requesting revisions indefinitely to stall. Neither is prevented here.
If it happens, a cap on open versions per conversation is the first lever.
